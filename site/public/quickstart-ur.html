From the kit to the first pick on a Universal Robots arm: mount the camera, connect the camera computer, install the Perceptronic URCap, calibrate, and add the 3D Pick node to a program. Steps 1 to 4 are done once per robot.

Beta software. The integrator is responsible for the risk assessment and safety of the cell. Run the first picks at reduced speed, with the area clear and a hand on the emergency stop. If a step here does not match what you see, write to nick@advin.io.

0What you need

RobotA Universal Robots e-Series arm on PolyScope {{PS5_RANGE}}, or an arm on PolyScope X {{PSX_RANGE}}.
GripperAny gripper with its own open and close program nodes. Perceptronics never drives it.
KitRealSense D435 camera, camera bracket and bolts, camera computer with its power supply, high-flex USB 3 cable, Ethernet cable.
USB stickFormatted FAT32 (most sticks up to 32 GB are). The pendant does not read exFAT or NTFS.
The URCap{{PS5_FILE}} for PolyScope 5 or {{PSX_FILE}} for PolyScope X, from perceptronics.advin.io.
A laptopWith SSH, only to set up the camera computer (step 2) and to calibrate (step 5).

1Mount the camera

  1. Fit the bracket between the robot's tool flange and the gripper, using the gripper's own bolts. Use the bracket made for your flange: ISO 9409-1-50-4-M6 for the e-Series, or the UR20 version.
  2. Screw the D435 to the bracket with its 1/4-20 and M3 screws.
  3. Run the USB 3 cable along the arm with strain relief at the wrist. Jog the wrist through its full rotation and check that the cable does not snag or pull.

Re-mounting the bracket or the camera later means calibrating again (step 5).

2Connect the camera computer

CableFrom → to
USB 3D435 → a blue USB 3 port on the camera computer. Straight in, no hub.
EthernetCamera computer → the robot controller's Ethernet port, or the cell switch the robot is on.
PowerThe kit's supply. A weaker supply browns out the camera: on a Raspberry Pi 5 that is the 27 W (5.1 V, 5 A) supply.

Power-up order does not matter. Give the computer a minute after power-on. It runs the camera software as a service, gives itself 192.168.3.20 on the robot's cable, and gives the robot 192.168.3.3 if the robot asks for an address (DHCP). It stays quiet if another DHCP server is already on that network.

Setting up a camera computer yourself

Skip this if yours came set up. Flash Raspberry Pi OS Lite (64-bit) or Debian arm64 with a user and your laptop's SSH key, connect it to the internet for the first install, then from a checkout of the repository on the laptop:

scripts/deploy-pi.sh <user>@<computer-address> --cell ur3 --robot-host 192.168.3.3

Use --cell ur3 for an e-Series arm or --cell ur20 for a UR20. The first run builds the camera library: about 10 minutes on a Raspberry Pi 5, longer on a Pi 4. It ends with a check of camera, network, robot, calibration and tool length, each failure with its fix.

Tool length. The camera computer approaches by the fingertips. Set the distance from the flange face to the fingertips, in metres, as PERCEPTRONICS_TIP_M in /etc/perceptronics/cell.env (and UR_ROBOT_MODEL to your arm), then sudo systemctl restart perceptronics-cockpit. The UR3e profile's 0.163 is a Robotiq Hand-E on a 6 mm adapter.

3Install the URCap

PolyScope 5

  1. Copy {{PS5_FILE}} to the top level of the stick (not in a folder; do not rename it). Eject the stick properly before pulling it out.
  2. Plug it into the pendant. ☰ → Settings → System → URCaps.
  3. Tap +, select the file, tap Open. If a Mac wrote the stick, pick the file without a leading ._.
  4. Tap Restart when PolyScope asks.

Or let the stick install it: put urmagic_perceptronic.sh from the URCap's GitHub release beside it, turn on Settings → Security → General → Run magic files, power the arm off and plug the stick in. The pendant shows ! USB ! and the robot restarts by itself.

PolyScope X

  1. Copy {{PSX_FILE}} to the stick and plug it into the pendant.
  2. ☰ → System Manager → URCaps, and unlock it with the admin password.
  3. Add a URCap and select the file.
  4. When it is listed, ☰ → Reload. Perceptronic now appears under Application.

Updating from a URCap called RealSense Pilot: remove it first (select it, tap −). It is a different bundle; its saved settings do not carry over.

4Point the robot at the camera computer

  1. Robot network. ☰ → Settings → System → Network → DHCP → Apply. The camera computer gives the robot 192.168.3.3. A robot that must keep a static address: put it on 192.168.3.x / 255.255.255.0.
  2. PolyScope 5: Installation tab → URCaps → Perceptronic. The Cockpit field already reads 192.168.3.20 and the live picture appears within seconds. A camera computer at another address: type it and tap Save. Then ☰ → Save Installation.
  3. PolyScope X: Application → Perceptronic. Check the Cockpit address (192.168.3.20) and tap Save.

No picture? The node says what to check first: the Ethernet link lights, the computer's power, the robot's network settings, or the camera's USB cable.

5Calibrate the camera to the flange

Calibration finds where the camera sits on the wrist. Without it, positions come from the bracket's drawing and can be off by a few centimetres. Do it once after mounting, and again whenever the bracket or camera has moved, or picks land consistently off by more than a few millimetres.

  1. Switch the pendant to Remote control (top right): calibrating moves the arm over the network.
  2. Put one part on the table under the camera, about 0.3 m below it.
  3. From the laptop: 
    ssh <user>@<computer-address> sudo -u perceptronics /opt/perceptronics/current/bin/perceptronics \
    --cell /etc/perceptronics/cell.env calibrate --cockpit http://127.0.0.1:7621 --apply
    The arm orbits the part slowly (39 views at three distances, a few minutes). The result takes effect at once and is kept through restarts and updates.
  4. Switch the pendant back to Local if that is how the cell runs. The 3D Pick node runs in either mode.

6Teach pick areas (optional)

A pick area tells the node which part of the table to look at. In the Perceptronic installation (PolyScope 5) or application (PolyScope X) node, on Pick areas: tap New area and touch the table with the fingertips at a corner, along one edge and on the far side. The map shows each area against the arm's reach. Without an area, the node finds the table by itself.

7Write the first pick program

The 3D Pick node is one line with no children. It looks, finds the next part, and ends with the fingertips at the grip. It does not touch the gripper, so your gripper's nodes go around it. Program tab, inside a loop:

Open gripper        ← your gripper's node
3D Pick             ← Program → URCaps → 3D Pick
Close gripper       ← your gripper's node
If rs_pick_found    ← lift, place
  1. Picture point. Move the arm so the camera looks down at the parts from 0.3 to 0.6 m and tap +. Add more (up to 12) to cover a larger area; tap a point's second line to choose the pick area it looks at. On PolyScope X, open the node's screen with Teach & options…
  2. Pick order. Tap a tile: by rows or by columns, from any corner.
  3. Options → Part. Box, or cylinder standing on its end; its length, width and height as it lies; the tolerance (default 25 %).
  4. Options → Approach. How far above the part the fingertips stop first (25 mm), the grip depth, the finger room the grip check wants on each side (20 mm), whether a box is gripped across its long side, and the closer look.
  5. Check approach opens PolyScope's move screen over the first part: hold Move and watch the fingertips arrive above it.

The node's picture shows the parts it will pick in green, numbered in pick order, and parts that nearly match in yellow, each with the reason: off size, two parts touching, out of reach, no room for a finger, outside the pick area, cut off by the edge of the picture. The toggle in its corner switches to the depth view. rs_pick_found is True once the tool is at the grip; rs_pick_loc is the picture point the part came from.

8When it does not work

You seeCheck
No answer from the camera computerIts power and Ethernet link lights; the robot's network on 192.168.3.x / 255.255.255.0; the address in the Cockpit field.
A computer answers, but not the camera programOn the camera computer: systemctl status perceptronics-cockpit and journalctl -u perceptronics-cockpit -n 50.
The camera gives no pictureThe D435 in a blue port, no hub; the kit's power supply. Re-plug the camera: the picture comes back by itself.
Parts drawn, but picks miss by centimetresCalibrate (step 5), and check the tool length (step 2).
A part is yellowRead its reason. Off size: check the size and tolerance. Out of reach: move the parts or the picture point. No room for a finger: space the parts further apart.
A "3D Pick: no pick" popupIt names the reason; the node's picture shows what it saw.
The robot got no addressAnother DHCP server is on that network, so the camera computer stays quiet. Give the robot a static 192.168.3.3.

A full check from the laptop: ssh -t <user>@<computer-address> sudo perceptronics-doctor. Every failing line carries its fix.
