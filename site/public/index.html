Perceptronics Beta

3D picking for any industrial robot. Enter the part's size; the robot finds every one lying on a flat surface and puts the gripper on it.

A depth camera on the wrist and a small computer beside the robot find every part of that size on a table or tray, in any position and at any angle, number them in the pick order you choose, and bring the tool to the grip on each one. Your program opens and closes the gripper. The robot side is a connector that runs in the robot's own programming environment: Universal Robots is the first.

When to use it

Use it where parts of one known size arrive on a flat surface but not in a known position.

Loose parts on a table or tray

Parts are set down by hand, in any position and at any angle. The robot finds each one instead of relying on a fixture or a taught grid.

Machine tending infeed

Blanks are placed roughly in front of a CNC or a press. The robot goes to the next blank; the rest of your program loads it.

Instead of a fixture or pallet pattern

When the part changes, enter its new length, width and height on the teach pendant. There is no nest to machine and no pattern to re-teach.

Not for

  • Bin picking: parts piled on or leaning against each other. Parts must lie separately on a flat surface.
  • Shapes other than boxes and cylinders standing on end.
  • Transparent or mirror-like parts that a depth camera cannot see.
  • Parts closer than 0.25 m to the camera.
  • Telling apart parts of the same size. It measures; it does not read colour, labels or markings.

Connectors

The camera, the computer and the detection are the same for every arm. A connector puts picking into the robot's own programs.

Re-flashing a camera computer

Kits ship set up. To start a Raspberry Pi 5 camera computer over on a fresh card, use Raspberry Pi Imager (raspberrypi.com/software) with this site's image list:

open -a "Raspberry Pi Imager" --args --repo https://perceptronics.advin.io/imager.json

That is macOS; on Windows or Linux, start Imager with the same --repo option. Choose Raspberry Pi 5, Perceptronics pick PC and the card, and write it. There is nothing to configure: the card boots as a camera computer at 192.168.3.20. Image of {{PICKPC_DATE}}: {{PICKPC_FILE}}.

The kit comes from Advin. To ask for one, write to nick@advin.io with your robot model, controller version and gripper.
