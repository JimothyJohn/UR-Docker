3D picking off flat surfaces

3D picking for any industrial robot. A depth camera on the wrist and a small computer beside the robot find every part of the size you enter lying on a flat surface, number them in the pick order you choose, and bring the tool to the grip on each one. A connector puts this into the robot's own programs; the robot's program opens and closes the gripper.

Planning figures, not guarantees. The numbers below are deliberately conservative estimates derived from the software's limits, its programmed speeds and the camera's noise. This release is a beta and the figures have not been measured on a production cell. Test with your parts, gripper and layout before you commit to them.

Not for

  • Bin picking: piled or leaning parts
  • Shapes other than boxes and upright cylinders
  • Transparent or mirror-like parts
  • Telling apart parts of the same size
  • Driving the gripper or the place move

Capabilities

  • Finds parts by volume, from depth alone: boxes, and cylinders standing on end. Colour and lighting contrast are not used.
  • Any position, any rotation on the surface. Fingers close across the short side, or the long side of a box if you choose.
  • Eight pick orders: by rows or by columns, from any corner.
  • Checks before it moves: reach by the arm's kinematics, and room for the fingers beside the part.
  • Says why not: rejected parts are drawn in yellow with the reason (off size, touching, out of reach, no finger room, outside the area, cut off).
  • Queues what it saw: the next part is picked without a trip back to the picture point.
  • Runs inside the robot's own program through its connector: one step that ends with the tool at the grip. No internet needed.
