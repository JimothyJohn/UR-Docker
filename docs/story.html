The Little Robot
Who Learned to Help

Born in a workshop on a winter's day · 15 December 2021

Chapter One

In which a robot is born, knowing nothing at all

❀

Once upon a time, in a tidy little workshop where the frost drew ferns on the windows, a maker named Nick bolted together a robot from six round joints and a handful of bright wires. It had a long, gentle arm, a curious tilting head, and a heart that beat in tiny electric pulses.

But on the day it woke, the little robot could do nothing. It did not know what a part was, nor how heavy, nor how tall. It did not know where to reach, or where to set things down. It knew only one thing in all the world — how to ask a question, very politely.

“Have you already taught me what to do?” — the robot's very first words

And because nobody had, the little robot did the bravest thing a robot can do. It decided to learn.

Chapter Two

In which the robot asks a great many questions

✦

Before it could lift a single thing, the robot wanted to understand the world it had been given. So it asked, one question at a time, the way a child asks about everything under the sun.

“How much does your part weigh?”
“How tall is it? How wide?”
“How many rows shall we make? How many columns?”
“Which wire is my gripper on?”
“Which little light tells me the part is there?”

For every answer, the robot nodded its bright antenna and tucked the number away somewhere safe inside, so it would never have to ask twice.

Chapter Three

In which they hold hands, and the robot is taught

❦

Now came the gentlest magic of all. The maker took the robot softly by the wrist, and the robot went loose and trusting in his hands — this is a thing robots call freedrive, but it is really just holding hands.

Together they moved through the air. “This is where we pick the part up,” said the maker, and the robot remembered the spot. With a hopeful little warning it opened its fingers wide:

“Hold this part — I'm about to drop it!”

They found a safe place to pass through in the middle, and a place to set the part down, and at last a corner of the table where neat parts could be stacked in tidy rows and columns, like loaves cooling on a shelf.

Chapter Four

In which the robot tries, and tries again

✿

The little robot was eager, but it was not yet proud. After every lesson it would lift the part all by itself — “Alright, let me try!” — and then, with its head tilted just so, it would ask the most honest question a learner can ask:

“Did I pick it up okay?”
“Did I place it okay?” — asked after every single attempt

If the answer was no, the robot did not sulk and did not give up. It simply loosened its wrist, took its maker's hand again, and learned the spot anew — around and around, as many times as it took, until at last the answer was yes. That patience was built into its very bones.

Chapter Five

In which the little robot becomes a great help

❀

And then one quiet morning the lessons were done, and the work began. When a little light blinked to say a part is ready, the robot swept down, gathered it up, carried it gently past the safe middle place, and set it where it belonged. With a wink of its signal it told the machine, “your turn,” and waited ever so patiently for the machine to finish.

Then it gathered the finished piece and laid it in the pallet — row by row, column by column — until the whole tray was filled with neat and tidy parts. And it never grew tired, and it never grew bored, and it never once forgot a single thing it had been taught.

· and so ·

The End — but not really

✦

The little robot had only one last wish, and it asked for it the way it asked for everything — kindly, and out loud:

“Make sure to save this,
so that I can remember it!”

For a helpful little robot is not born helpful. It is born curious, and it becomes helpful one patient question at a time — held by a kind hand, willing to try again, and trusted to remember.
